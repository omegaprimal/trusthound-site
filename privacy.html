Privacy policy

Effective November 4, 2026

The short version: Trust Hound has no tracking and no ads. When it checks a post, it sends that post's text, captions, a couple of video still frames and a text version of what was said to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI. With the free version that goes straight from your browser using your own API key. With Trust Hound Pro it passes through our server, which counts your checks and doesn't keep the post. Video sound is turned into text on your own computer and never uploaded. Your settings and results stay in your browser. We never sell your data.

Who we are

Trust Hound is a Chrome extension that flags false and misleading claims in social media posts and videos. In this policy, "we" means the developer of Trust Hound. You can reach us at support@trusthound.dog.

What Trust Hound reads

Trust Hound only runs on the sites you allow in its settings (Facebook, X, Instagram, Threads, TikTok, Reddit and YouTube by default). On those sites, when a post stays on your screen for about a second, Trust Hound reads:

  • The visible text of that post, such as its caption or message.
  • The web address a post links to, if it shares a link to an article or website.
  • Up to two of the post's own pictures, so that text inside them (a screenshot of a headline, a meme caption, a chart) can be read. Profile pictures and icons are left out.
  • For videos, any captions shown while it plays, and up to two still images from the video so that words shown on screen can be read.
  • For videos, if voice listening is on, the video's own sound for up to about 12 seconds while it plays.

It does not read your private messages, your passwords, forms you fill out, or pages on other websites. It never uses your microphone.

How video sound is handled

When voice listening is on, Trust Hound turns a video's speech into text using a speech recognition model that runs entirely on your computer. The audio is never uploaded or saved. It is discarded as soon as it has been turned into text. Only that text is used in the check described below.

Some sites, such as Instagram, play videos straight from their media servers in a way that stops extensions from reading the video's sound or pictures on the page. For those videos, Trust Hound downloads the start of the same video file (the part your browser is already playing) from the site's media server, takes the speech and two still images from it on your computer, and then discards the file. Nothing about you is sent with that download.

The first time listening is used, Trust Hound downloads the speech model (Whisper, made available on Hugging Face) once and stores it in your browser. That download doesn't include any information about you. You can turn listening off in settings at any time.

Where that information goes

To check a post, Trust Hound sends the post text, captions, still images, the post's pictures and the text of what was said (never the audio itself), plus the name of the site, to Anthropic's Claude API. Anthropic's AI decides whether the post contains a checkable claim and, if it does, searches the web for reliable sources and returns a verdict.

When a post links to an article, Anthropic's AI may open that public article once to see whether the post describes it accurately. Trust Hound keeps short lists of known satire sites and of sites fact-checkers have repeatedly flagged, and mentions a match to the AI; the lists are built into the extension and nothing is looked up elsewhere.

When a picture can't be read on your computer because the site doesn't allow it, Trust Hound sends Anthropic the picture's public web address instead, and Anthropic's servers open that picture directly. That address points to the picture only, not to you or your account.

Trust Hound does not send the address of the page, your name, your account details on the social site, or your browsing history.

If you use your own Claude API key (free)

Checks go directly from your browser to Anthropic using the API key you created and entered yourself. They don't pass through any server we run.

If you use Trust Hound Pro (paid, or the free trial)

Checks go from your browser to the Trust Hound Pro server, which we run on Cloudflare, and from there to Anthropic using our own API key. The server:

  • Passes the post's content straight through to Anthropic and back, and does not store it.
  • Confirms you have an active trial or subscription by looking up your ExtensionPay ID (a random code created for your copy of Trust Hound) with ExtensionPay.
  • Keeps a count of the checks you've used this month, any extra checks you've bought, and a one-way scrambled code (a hash) of your ExtensionPay ID and of your trial email address, so the trial can only be used once per email. It does not store your email address, your name, or the posts you read.

Payments are handled by ExtensionPay and Stripe. When you start a trial or subscribe, you give your email address and payment details to them, not to us; we never see your card number. ExtensionPay tells Trust Hound whether your subscription is active. If you buy extra checks, Stripe tells our server that the purchase succeeded using a random buyer code, so the checks can be added to your account. ExtensionPay's privacy policy is at extensionpay.com/privacy and Stripe's is at stripe.com/privacy.

Post content goes only to Anthropic (through our server for Pro). Anthropic handles this data under its own terms and privacy policy, which you can read at anthropic.com/legal/privacy.

What is stored on your computer

Trust Hound uses your browser's local extension storage to keep:

  • Your settings, including your Claude API key if you use one, which sites are on, and your daily spending cap.
  • Your Pro status, if you use Pro: your plan, checks left and the date they refill.
  • Recent results for up to about 400 posts, so the same post isn't checked and paid for twice. This includes the verdict, a short summary of the claims, and source links.
  • Spending totals for today and overall, so the extension can enforce your daily cap.

This data stays in your browser on this computer and we can't access it. Your API key is only ever sent to Anthropic, as part of check requests. If you start a trial or subscribe, ExtensionPay saves your ExtensionPay ID in Chrome's synced extension storage, so your subscription follows you to other computers where you're signed in to Chrome.

What we don't do

  • We don't collect analytics, usage statistics or crash reports.
  • We don't collect your name, and we don't keep your email address. (If you use Pro, ExtensionPay has it, for your subscription.)
  • We don't sell, rent or share your data with anyone, and we don't use it for advertising.
  • We don't use your data to decide whether you're creditworthy or for lending purposes.

Chrome Web Store user data policy

Trust Hound's use of information received from Chrome extension APIs follows the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Page content is used only to provide the extension's single purpose, checking claims in posts you view, and is not transferred to anyone other than Anthropic (through our Pro server, for Pro users) for that purpose.

Your choices

  • Turn off any site, or turn off video checking, still images or voice listening, in Trust Hound's settings.
  • Switch to manual mode so nothing is checked until you tap "Check this."
  • Use "Clear saved results" in settings to delete stored results.
  • Uninstall the extension to remove all of its stored data, including your API key, from your browser.
  • Delete or replace your API key at any time in your Anthropic account.
  • Cancel Pro any time from "Manage subscription" in Trust Hound's settings. To have your check counts deleted from our server, email us.

Children

Trust Hound is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If Trust Hound changes how it handles data, we'll update this policy before that change takes effect and change the date at the top.
