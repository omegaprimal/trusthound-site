How it sniffs things out

You scroll the way you always do. Trust Hound only speaks up when something deserves a closer look.

  1. It notices what you stop on

    Posts you scroll right past are ignored. When you pause on one, Trust Hound reads the text, any words in its pictures, and where its links go. For videos it also reads the captions and any words on screen, and listens to what's said, right on your computer.

  2. It sorts what it finds

    Opinions, jokes and politics are left alone. Claims about health, money, scams and safety get checked right away. Everything else gets a badge you can tap.

  3. It checks and shows its work

    Trust Hound searches reliable sources and puts a badge on the post. Tap the badge for a plain-English explanation and links to every source.

What the badges mean

A badge is a nudge to look closer, not the final word. Every one comes with its sources.

❌ FalseReliable sources clearly say otherwise.
⚠️ MisleadingPartly true, but missing context that changes the meaning, or exaggerated.
🟡 MixedSome claims in the post hold up and some don't.
⚠️ Unproven claimThe only support is the seller's own marketing or numbers. Common in ads.
✅ Checks outReliable sources back it up.
❓ Can't verifyToo new or too little evidence to say. Trust Hound won't guess.
🔍 Claim foundA claim on a lower-stakes topic, like a fun science fact. Tap it if you want it checked.
👤 Personal claimSomething only the poster can prove, like "I make $1,000 a week." Trust Hound tells you if the post is also selling something.
🐕 SatireThe post links to a known satire site, like The Onion. It's a joke on purpose, so it isn't called false.

Badges also carry a tag when money is involved: Ad for paid ads, and Selling for posts pushing a product, course or "link in bio" without an ad label.

It checks the ads, too

Ads are where a lot of the worst claims live: miracle products, fake celebrity endorsements, get-rich schemes. Trust Hound always checks posts and videos marked Sponsored or Ad, tags them clearly, and points out how the ad is trying to sell to you, like fake urgency or testimonials nobody can verify.

It makes ads obvious, even when the platform doesn't. Social sites keep shrinking their ad labels into tiny gray text. Trust Hound puts a clear Ad tag right on the badge, and flags posts that are quietly selling something with no label at all.

It follows the link

A post can say one thing while the article it links to says something else. When a post shares a link, Trust Hound opens the article and checks whether the post describes it honestly. A scary headline over a calm article gets called misleading.

It also knows the difference between a joke and a hoax. Links to satire sites like The Onion get a "Satire" badge instead of a verdict, and links to sites that fact-checkers have caught publishing hoaxes again and again get a heads-up.

What it leaves alone

Trust Hound is here to catch hoaxes and scams, not to argue with you.

Politics

Posts about elections, politicians, parties and hot-button issues are skipped by default. No badge, no check.

Opinions and jokes

Your uncle's hot take, memes and satire aren't claims of fact, so they don't get a badge.

Comments and friends' moments

Trust Hound checks posts, not the comments under them. Friends' Stories, Marketplace listings and search results are left alone too.

Your private stuff

No account, no tracking, no ads. Trust Hound doesn't collect your browsing or sell anything about you.
